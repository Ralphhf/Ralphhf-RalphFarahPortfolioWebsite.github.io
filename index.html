Systems I built end to end.

All of these are mine from scratch: the data source, the pipeline, the storage, the scoring, the interface. I build them with AI as the pair — and in most of them an LLM is also a component inside the system, doing classification, extraction or vision work that regex can't. Directing that well is its own skill, and it's the reason one person can ship this much.

Market Catalyst Detector

Running

A service that reads financial news the second it publishes, scores each headline through 400+ regex rules plus an LLM classifier, and pushes ranked alerts to Discord and an Interactive Brokers paper-trading bot. The exit strategy wasn't a guess — it came out of backtesting 50,000+ historical trades against second-level price bars.

Python · SQLite · Claude API · Polygon · IBKR · DigitalOcean

About New Brunswick, NJ

Ralph Farah

I'm a Business Intelligence Developer at W. R. Berkley, working on the reporting stack for a commercial insurance carrier. My day is SSIS packages, stored procedures, SSRS reports and Power BI models — and the reconciliation checks that sit between them.

I came in as an intern on the same team, building Power Apps and Power Automate tooling and C# / ASP.NET MVC portals to move data between internal systems and the warehouse, and moved into the BI work from there.

Outside of that I build complete systems on my own — scrapers, pipelines, scoring engines and the interfaces on top of them. I work with AI heavily and deliberately: as the tool I write with, and as a component inside what I ship when the problem needs judgment rather than rules. Knowing where a model belongs in a system, and where it doesn't, is most of the value.
